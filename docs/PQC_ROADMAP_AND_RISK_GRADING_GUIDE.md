# QuarkShield — PQC Roadmap Report & Risk Grading Guide

A reference for **every section** of the PQC Executive Roadmap report and for **how the risk
numbers are computed**. Two scoring systems exist and measure different things — don't conflate
them:

| # | Where it shows | What it measures | File |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | PQC Roadmap report (letter grade A–F) | **Quantum-readiness** of the environment | `server/src/lib/roadmapReport.ts` |
| 2 | Threat & Risk Graph (Residual Risk High/Med/Low, Quantum Risk Rating 0–100) | **Threat-model residual risk** (likelihood × impact per threat) | `server/src/lib/threatGraph/scoring.ts` |
| 3 | Post-Quantum Guard app (per-host scan grade) | A single host's local scan | agent |

---

## Part A — PQC Roadmap Report, section by section

The report is generated by `roadmapReport.ts` (data) + `reportController.ts` (HTML render),
scoped to one tenant or the whole fleet. Open it from the tenant portal: **Cryptographic BOM →
View PQC Roadmap** (opens HTML in a new tab).

### 1. Header & Executive Summary
- *"<org> has N catalogued cryptographic assets, of which M (X%) are quantum-vulnerable."*
- **Quantum-vulnerable** = breakable by a cryptographically-relevant quantum computer: asymmetric
  crypto (RSA, ECDSA, ECDH, DH, DSA) via **Shor**. Symmetric (AES-256) and SHA-2/3 are *not*
  counted as vulnerable (Grover only halves strength → still safe). This is the `is_vulnerable`
  flag on each asset.

### 2. Risk Grade (A–F)  ← see Part B for the exact formula
A single letter summarizing quantum-readiness, driven mostly by the vulnerable-asset percentage.

### 3. Mosca Verdict (X + Y > Z)
Michele Mosca's theorem: **if (time your data must stay secret, X) + (time to migrate, Y) >
(time until a quantum computer exists, Z), you are already too late — start now.**
- The report states ACTION REQUIRED when X+Y > Z given the vulnerable share and multi-year
  migration timelines. `Y` is inflated by hard-to-migrate assets (code-signing / roots of trust).

### 4. Cryptographic Purpose Breakdown
*What the vulnerable crypto is used for* — because "a list of algorithms is not an inventory."
Each asset is classified at scan time (`cryptoPurpose.ts → classifyPurpose()`) into a **purpose**,
and grouped here with its migration **urgency** and NIST target:

| Purpose | Threat urgency | NIST target |
|---|---|---|
| `key_establishment`, `rsa_key_transport` | **Immediate — Harvest-Now-Decrypt-Later** (traffic captured today is decrypted once a QC exists) | FIPS 203 (ML-KEM) |
| `tls_certificate`, `code_or_data_signing`, `ca_trust_anchor`, `ssh_authentication` | **Planned — signature-forgery risk** (only matters once a QC exists; no retroactive break) | FIPS 204 (ML-DSA) / 205 (SLH-DSA) |
| `symmetric`, `configuration` | **Hardening** (not quantum-broken) | — |
| `unknown` / `unclassified` | **Review** (couldn't classify, or not yet re-scanned) | — |

> **Note:** assets scanned *before* this feature deployed show as **Unclassified** (`purpose = NULL`,
> legacy-safe) until re-scanned. Re-run a scan to populate it (classification is server-side at
> ingest — no agent change needed).

### 5. Migration Size Impact — "What Breaks"
*The problem nobody else flags:* PQC keys/signatures are **much larger**, so they can break
size-constrained systems. Engine: `sizeImpact.ts`.
- Exact NIST sizes: ML-DSA-65 signature **3309 B** (vs Ed25519 64 B, ~52×); SLH-DSA-128s **7856 B**;
  ML-KEM-768 ek+ct **2272 B** (vs X25519 32 B, ~37×).
- Checked against real limits: firmware signature slot (512 B), smartcard APDU (255 B), TLS cert
  chain (16 KB), QUIC amplification (3600 B), MTU fragmentation (1400 B).
- Verdict per item: **breaks** / **tight** (>80% of limit) / **ok**; severity **blocker** /
  **degraded** / **advisory**.
- **This section only renders when there are signing- or key-exchange-classified assets** (i.e.
  after a re-scan populates `purpose`). The per-priority "What breaks if migrated: X → Y · N×
  larger" lines render always (computed from algorithm alone).
- **Honesty caveat (built in):** sizes are exact, but the limits are *nominal* and the findings are
  *conditional* — QuarkShield does not yet measure each device's actual slot/MTU, so these are
  "where it will break if these typical limits apply," not a measured failure.

### 6. Top Migration Priorities
The highest-residual-risk assets to migrate first, each annotated with its size impact
("What breaks if migrated…"). Ordered by risk, then by migration friction.

### 7. Remediation Recommendations
Concrete fixes keyed to each finding type (e.g. legacy TLS → TLS 1.3 + hybrid X25519MLKEM768;
RSA signing → ML-DSA; firmware → SLH-DSA with the APDU/off-card workarounds).

### 8. Migration Roadmap (phases)
Time-boxed phases mapped to the federal mandates:
- **Phase 1** — Discover & prioritize (now).
- **Phase 2** — Crypto-agility & hybrid TLS (2025–2027, CNSA 2.0 begin).
- **Phase 3** — Signing, firmware & PKI (2027–2030, CNSA 2.0 software/firmware signing).
- **Phase 4** — Full CNSA 2.0 (by 2030/2035 deadlines).

## Part B — Risk Grading, exactly how

### B.1  PQC Roadmap letter grade (A–F)
`roadmapReport.ts`:
```
blended = round( vulnPct × 0.7  +  avgRisk × 0.3 )
```
- **vulnPct** (70%) = % of catalogued crypto assets that are quantum-vulnerable.
- **avgRisk** (30%) = average per-host risk score (0–100; CVEs, config, exposure).

| blended | grade |
|---|---|
| ≥ 65 | **F** |
| ≥ 50 | D+ |
| ≥ 35 | C |
| ≥ 15 | B+ |
| > 0 | A− |
| 0 | A |

It is **vulnerability-dominant** on purpose: when most of your crypto is Shor-breakable, PQC
readiness is failing regardless of host hygiene. (At 96% vulnerable, `96 × 0.7 = 67.2 ≥ 65 → F`.)

### B.2  Threat & Risk Graph scoring (`scoring.ts`)
Per-threat, not per-algorithm.

**Inherent risk = Likelihood × Impact**, each on a 1–4 scale (product 1–16):
- **Likelihood (1–4)** — how plausible the threat is, adjusted by **exploitation intelligence**:
  CISA **KEV** (known-exploited → prioritized, carries the CISA due date) and **EPSS** (FIRST's
  exploit-probability score → nudges likelihood up).
- **Impact (1–4)** = `impactOf(asset) = max(asset.criticality, dataClassScore)`, capped 1–4, where
  data classification scores **public 1 / internal 2 / sensitive 3 / CUI 4**. So a CUI or
  mission-critical asset carries impact 4.

**Risk level** from the score: `≥12 critical · ≥6 high · ≥3 medium · else low`.

**Residual risk (After Remediation)** = `residualLikelihood × Impact`:
- Residual likelihood drops **only** as remediations are marked **done/verified**
  (`residualLikelihood = likelihood − round(fractionResolved × (likelihood−1))`).
- **Config presence is never credited as an effective control** — that requires RMF/MGC evidence.
  This is why a fresh environment shows *"Residual Risk: High · no remediation credited yet"*:
  nothing has been remediated, so residual = inherent.

**Quantum Risk Rating (0–100)** = `overallRiskScore` — a roll-up weighted toward the worst risks:
```
score = round( 100 × (0.6 × worst + 0.4 × avg_of_top10) / 16 )
```
Takes the top-10 residual scores: 60% the single worst + 40% their average, normalized by the
16 max. So one critical asset moves the needle a lot; a long tail of lows does not.

**The Overview tiles:**
- *Total Threats Identified* — count of distinct threats (+ attack paths).
- *High / Critical Risks* — risks whose level is high or critical.
- *Affected Controls* — distinct NIST 800-53/171 controls touched by open risks.
- *Residual Risk (After Remediation)* — the environment's residual level (High/Med/Low).
- *Quantum Risk Rating* — the 0–100 `overallRiskScore` above.

### Why the two grades don't match
The **roadmap grade** answers *"how much of my crypto is quantum-breakable"* (readiness). The
**threat-graph residual risk** answers *"given likelihood × impact and what I've remediated, how
much risk remains."* An environment can be 96% quantum-vulnerable (roadmap **F**) yet show a
particular residual level depending on asset criticality and remediation — they're complementary,
not redundant.
